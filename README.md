# COHORT ANALYSIS & CUSTOMER RETENTION 

1. Executive Summary

    This analysis examined customer transaction data to evaluate long-term business performance. By segmenting customers into monthly acquisition  cohorts, the project identified key trends in customer retention, revenue generation, and spending behavior. The findings provided a clear       baseline for evaluating customer lifetime value (CLV) and identified critical areas for retention strategy improvements.

2. Methodology

    Data Processing: Raw transaction data was cleaned and transformed using Python (Pandas).

    Cohort Segmentation: Customers were grouped into cohorts based on their initial transaction month.

    Metric Development: The analysis calculated:

  a.Retention Rates: The percentage of active customers per cohort over time.

  b.Average Revenue Per User (ARPU): Normalized revenue per cohort.

  c.Spend Band Analysis: Categorization of customers based on total historical spend.

3. Key Findings

    Retention Decay: The retention heatmaps revealed a consistent decline in active users across all cohorts after the initial acquisition month. This indicated that while initial conversion was strong, long-term engagement decreased significantly, suggesting a potential gap in the customer onboarding or post-purchase loyalty experience.

    Revenue Concentration: Total cohort revenue was heavily driven by the initial acquisition cohorts (specifically 2010-12), while subsequent monthly cohorts showed lower total revenue contributions. This highlighted a dependency on early customers for sustained revenue.

    Customer Value Distribution: The "Spend Band" analysis indicated that the majority of customers fell into lower spending tiers (101–500), while a smaller, highly valuable segment (5000+ spend) accounted for a disproportionate share of total revenue. This pointed to an opportunity for targeted VIP loyalty programs.

    Seasonality and Behavioral Trends: Variations in ARPU and revenue across different cohorts suggested that specific marketing campaigns or seasonal periods significantly influenced customer spend, rather than consistent, organic growth.

4. Recommendations

    Implement Targeted Retention Campaigns: Given the identified drop-off patterns, marketing efforts should be prioritized for customers between months 2 and 4 to boost long-term retention.

    Optimize High-Value Segments: With a clear concentration of revenue within the top spending tiers, the business should develop personalized experiences or exclusive offerings for these high-value customers to maximize their lifetime value.

    Investigate Early Cohort Success: The high performance of the 2010-12 cohort should be further investigated to determine if it was driven by specific product offerings, acquisition channels, or promotional strategies that could be replicated for new cohorts.
